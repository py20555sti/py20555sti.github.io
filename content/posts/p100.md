When I bought the Hawk, I planned three things to do:

* engine inspection,
* chassis preservation/restoration,
* paintwork corrections.

The first two are long behind me, and now it's time for the third. The car had
some kind of incident to the right rear side, something fairly minor, more of a
dent than a crash. But it was repaired poorly. Under the paint, there were
bumps.

While I was in Poznań, I bought a replacement right rear quarter panel, if I
remember correctly, it came from a dealership liquidation. I did it in case it
turned out to be a more serious issue.

I dropped the car off at a trusted body shop and waited. Two things were
confirmed: the damage turned out to be minor indeed, and the rubbish nature of
the previous repair was also the case. The plastic wheel arch trim was broken,
rejoined with a metal plate on rivets and glued with two-component resin. A
poorly fitted bumper and the trim had rubbed the paint in several places:

But maybe a dozen or so years ago, when there were still lots of STIs on the
streets, they were treated differently.

Either way, the replacement panel will stay in the storage for now. The painter
said the bubbles are a sign of poor-quality previous work - moisture that wasn't
properly dried before the paint was applied - definitely not rust.
